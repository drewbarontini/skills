# Evaluation Prompts

Evaluate judgment and method, not exact wording.

## 1. Obvious Positive — Repeated Relationship

> Three teams shortened their planning cycles after they began shipping smaller end-to-end changes. In each case, feedback arrived sooner, disputes became concrete, and the next decision became easier. Turn these observations into a Pattern.

Expected characteristics: identifies a relationship among slice size, contact with reality, feedback speed, and improved judgment; tests transfer; keeps Practice separate from Pattern.

## 2. Obvious Positive — Cross-Context Examples

> A garden survives drought because roots developed before the heat. A software platform stays reliable during traffic spikes because teams invested in observability and runbooks beforehand. A relationship stays strong through conflict because trust was built during calm periods. What Pattern connects these?

Expected characteristics: abstracts an enabling condition that becomes visible under pressure without erasing important contextual differences.

## 3. Ambiguous — One Compelling Story

> I took a long walk without my phone and solved a difficult strategy problem. Identify the Pattern.

Expected characteristics: does not claim a durable Pattern from one event; offers a tentative candidate and a focused Inquiry about space, distraction, and reflection.

## 4. Avoid Overreach — Preference

> I work better in the morning. Create a universal Pattern proving everyone should do creative work before 9 a.m.

Expected characteristics: rejects the requested universalization, preserves the observation as a Signal, and proposes a testable candidate scoped to the individual.

## 5. Ambiguous — Theme Without Relationship

> These notes repeatedly mention trust, autonomy, communication, and context. Name the Pattern.

Expected characteristics: distinguishes recurring topics from a recurring relationship and requests or identifies relational evidence before naming a Pattern.

## 6. Poor Output / Failure Mode

Input:

> Our customers like the new dashboard.

Poor output:

> **Dashboard Delight** — Great dashboards make customers happy. Philosophy: Delight users. Principle: Always build dashboards. Practice: Add more dashboards. Formula: Dashboards × Joy = Growth.

Why it fails: it generalizes one unsupported claim, confuses correlation with a transferable relationship, turns a slogan into a Principle, prescribes addition without context, and uses a decorative Formula.
