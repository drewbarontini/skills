# Model Mismatch

## Input

Support requests repeatedly say a control is “broken,” yet the control behaves according to its specification. In usability sessions, people also predict a different result before using it. A similar disagreement appears in internal training: operators describe the workflow according to the interface they expect, not the one implemented.

## Pattern

When feedback conflicts with observed system behavior, the conflict often reveals a gap between a person's mental model and the system's model.

## Philosophy

Feedback is evidence of how someone understands the system, not a specification for what the system should do. The mismatch must be understood before choosing whether to change the product, its explanation, or neither.

## Principle

Understand the gap before addressing the feedback.

## Practice

- Ask the person to predict what will happen, then compare that prediction with the actual behavior.
- Trace which concept, label, or prior convention produced the expectation.
- Correct the smallest source of mismatch instead of implementing each request literally.

## Evidence and Boundaries

- Originating examples: support reports, usability predictions, and internal training explanations.
- Transfer contexts: product interfaces, documented workflows, and operational systems.
- Conditions or limits: a conflict can still indicate a genuine defect; verify reality before diagnosing a mental-model gap.
- Confidence: supported.
