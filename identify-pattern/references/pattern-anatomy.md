# Pattern Anatomy

Use these distinctions when forming or reviewing a Pattern.

## Knowledge Layers

- **Signal** — a meaningful observation, tension, surprise, curiosity, or recurring experience.
- **Inquiry** — an active question worth investigating.
- **Pattern** — a recurring relationship that transfers across contexts.
- **Model** — a higher-order structure that organizes and explains related Patterns.
- **System** — an integrated body of philosophy, structure, and practice.

A Pattern should not be promoted into a Model or System. One Pattern may connect to existing Models or Systems as provenance.

## Pattern Layers

- **Pattern** describes what recurs. It is a claim about a relationship in reality.
- **Philosophy** explains why the relationship matters and how to regard it.
- **Principle** states what should generally guide judgment or action.
- **Practice** is a contextual expression of the Principle. Practices can change while the Principle remains useful.
- **Formula** compresses the relationship. It is optional and must clarify rather than decorate.

For example, the Equilio source describes Product Equilibrium this way:

- Pattern: products degrade when creating new value is not balanced with refining existing quality.
- Philosophy: equilibrium is responsive movement, not equal allocation or stasis.
- Principle: sense whether the product currently needs creation, refinement, or momentum.
- Practices: pair value and quality iterations, review the whole product, and simplify before adding.
- Formula: `Creation ↔ Refinement → Equilibrium`.

The layers support each other, but they are not interchangeable. “Review the whole product” is a Practice, not the Pattern. “Balance creation and refinement” is a Principle unless it explicitly describes the recurring relationship and its conditions.

## Evidence Test

Ask:

1. What happened in each concrete instance?
2. What relationship is shared across those instances?
3. Does the relationship appear outside the originating context?
4. Which conditions make it hold?
5. What counterexample would narrow or disprove it?
6. Is this genuinely recurring, or merely resonant wording?

One rich example can justify a **Candidate Pattern**, but not confident transferability. Do not invent extra examples to create the appearance of recurrence.

## Distillation Movement

The source material supports an iterative movement:

1. **Recognize** emerging recurrence and ask where else it appears.
2. **Reflect** on the relationship, its deeper meaning, and its connections or conflicts with existing knowledge.
3. **Repeat** the language in new contexts and refine it without losing meaning.

Naming happens after the relationship is understood. Clear, sticky language helps a Pattern travel; it does not make a weak observation transferable.
