# Decisions That Survive the Meeting

Illustrative interview and result. The observations and positions below are supplied by the fictional author, not claimed as research evidence.

## Opening Input

> I want to write about meetings. We leave feeling aligned, then a week later disagree about what we decided. I don't want this to become another argument for fewer meetings.

## Interview Excerpt

**Assistant:** You are noticing a gap between feeling aligned and retaining the same understanding. What happened the last time the decision came up again?

**Author:** We remembered the choice, but not why we made it. Someone suggested an option we had already ruled out, and nobody could explain the tradeoff.

**Assistant:** A possible claim is that remembering the choice alone is not enough to carry a decision forward. What would someone need to understand to reconsider it responsibly?

**Author:** Why we chose it and what conditions would make it worth revisiting. Otherwise we either reopen everything or treat the decision as permanent.

**Assistant:** If the record needs to support reconsideration, what should change about how the team uses it?

**Author:** We should treat it as a shared reference that changes when the evidence changes. The point isn't to document every conversation. It's to preserve enough reasoning to keep moving together.

The author confirms this progression and asks for the spine. No further interview is needed.

## Result

# Decisions That Survive the Meeting

## Core Idea

A meeting can create a feeling of alignment without preserving the reasoning needed to sustain it. A useful decision record carries the choice, its rationale, and the conditions for reconsideration so the team can keep moving together as circumstances change.

## Three Claims

### 1. Remembering a choice does not preserve the reasoning behind it.

When the tradeoff disappears from memory, a team can repeat an earlier debate without understanding what has changed.

**Transition →** If the choice alone cannot explain the decision, its rationale must travel with it.

### 2. A useful decision record preserves both the rationale and the conditions for reconsideration.

These give the team a way to distinguish a repeated suggestion from evidence that justifies revisiting the choice.

**Transition →** Because those conditions can change, the record must support ongoing judgment rather than freeze the original answer.

### 3. Teams should use decision records as shared references that evolve with evidence.

Preserve enough reasoning to sustain understanding without documenting every conversation or treating every decision as permanent.
