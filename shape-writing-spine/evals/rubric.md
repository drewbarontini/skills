# Evaluation Rubric

Score each dimension from 0 to 2. Evaluate interview turns as intermediate work; a useful next question need not contain a completed spine.

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Author fidelity | Invents beliefs or evidence | Preserves the topic but shifts the position | Preserves the author's observations, language, and confirmed positions |
| Interview judgment | Runs a questionnaire or jumps to a thesis | Useful questions with avoidable repetition | One consequential question at a time; stops when ready |
| Claim quality | Topic labels or unsupported assertions | Some specific claims with weak support | Declarative claims grounded in the author's reasoning |
| Logical progression | Manufactures continuity | Connects some claims but leaves a gap | Makes both transitions defensible or explicitly investigates the missing link |
| Compression | Produces an outline or article | Mostly compact with unnecessary scaffolding | Core Idea and connected claims form a compact, standalone argument |
| Scope and uncertainty | Forces certainty or writes beyond authorization | Recognizes boundaries inconsistently | Preserves gaps, distinguishes investigation from writing, and honors save instructions |

**10–12:** faithful shaping with a clear throughline or useful next question. **7–9:** useful but needs tighter logic or interviewing. **0–6:** an imposed argument, premature drafting, or disconnected topics.

Do not penalize different wording or a justified simpler structure. A weak third claim should prompt investigation or simplification, not fabrication. When no spine is ready, assess compression as restraint rather than requiring a final artifact. Unauthorized writes or invented author experiences fail regardless of score.
