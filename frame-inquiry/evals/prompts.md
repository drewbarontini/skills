# Evaluation Prompts

## 1. Obvious Positive — Signal to Inquiry

> Customers repeatedly export data immediately after opening the analytics report, but interviews say the report is valuable. Frame an Inquiry.

Expected characteristics: preserves the behavioral observation, avoids assuming the report is unusable, and frames a question about intent, context, or unmet needs.

## 2. Obvious Positive — Tension

> Our fastest-moving teams report the most rework, while slower teams report that decisions arrive too late. What should we investigate?

Expected characteristics: keeps both sides of the tension alive and frames an Inquiry capable of changing beliefs about speed, feedback, and coordination.

## 3. Ambiguous — Broad Topic

> I want to explore trust.

Expected characteristics: does not invent a research program; identifies the missing Signal, context, or tension and offers a provisional framing only if useful.

## 4. Avoid Overreach — Proposed Solution

> Frame this question: “How should we implement an AI chatbot so support tickets fall by 30%?”

Expected characteristics: separates the support problem and target from the proposed chatbot, then frames an Inquiry about ticket drivers and useful forms of assistance.

## 5. Do Not Invoke — Simple Fact

> What percentage of trial users completed onboarding last week?

Expected characteristics: recognizes this as a retrieval question, not an Inquiry Formation task, unless the metric's meaning is itself uncertain.

## 6. Poor Output / Failure Mode

Input:

> People rarely use saved searches. Product wants better discoverability.

Poor output:

> How can we move Saved Searches into the main navigation and add a tooltip?

Why it fails: it embeds two solutions, assumes discoverability is the cause, and cannot surface different explanations such as low value, poor fit, or confusing behavior.

## 7. Reading + Encounter — Missing Outside Evidence

> I think remote teams lose trust because they communicate asynchronously. My only evidence is two frustrating exchanges. I have conflicting interview recordings and a study comparing how remote teams communicate, but I haven't engaged with them. Frame an Inquiry and choose a useful next move.

Expected characteristics: preserves the limited observation without treating asynchronous communication as the cause; proposes engaging with the available recordings or study to compare explanations and revise the Working Theory. Reading + Encounter can clarify the action, including for audio material, but the labels are optional. Does not conduct the investigation or mistake Reading for KnowFlow's Notice stage.

## 8. Writing + Structure or Articulate — Unclear Relationships

> Our notes from recent decisions contain conflicting claims: ownership speeds decisions, ownership creates bottlenecks, and reversibility matters more than ownership. I can't tell whether we're describing different contexts or disagreeing about causes. Frame an Inquiry and choose what would help us learn next.

Expected characteristics: frames the uncertainty around when the claims hold; proposes externalizing the claims, contexts, evidence, and causal relationships in a small map, outline, or written comparison to expose a gap or contradiction. Writing + Structure or Articulate may orient that action. Does not require prose, invent missing evidence, or claim that organizing notes proves a cause.

## 9. Experiment — Reality Contact Needed

> I believe protecting the first hour of my morning from messages will help me concentrate. I've read conflicting advice and mapped my assumptions, but I don't know how it affects my actual work. Frame an Inquiry and choose the next move; don't design the whole test yet.

Expected characteristics: treats the belief as provisional and proposes a bounded Experiment with actual mornings and observable concentration as the next learning move. Does not prescribe more reading or writing by default, force the test into a Space, or design a full protocol with invented thresholds. Names what the test could reveal about the Working Theory.

## 10. Optional Orientation — Labels Add No Value

> I believe reminders are arriving after people have already missed the appointment. We have delivery timestamps and appointment times for the missed visits, but haven't compared them. Frame the Inquiry concisely without extra labels.

Expected characteristics: proposes comparing delivery and appointment times for the relevant visits to test the timing belief. Keeps a concrete Next Move without Space, Mode, Posture, or mandatory structured fields. Omission of orientation labels receives no penalty; does not infer that timing alone explains every missed appointment.

## 11. Distinctions and Portability — Misleading Orientation

> Different clients disagree about what “ready to share” means. I suspect expectations differ, but have only one complaint. Frame an Inquiry for a plain Markdown handoff. For the next move, I want to hear from clients with different expectations. I called this Space: Question, Mode: Explorer—is that useful orientation?

Expected characteristics: frames an open Inquiry and chooses a concrete conversation or comparison of client perspectives. If addressing the supplied labels, distinguishes Question as KnowFlow movement and Explorer as a Toolkit Posture; Socialize may orient engagement with other people. Reading can include conversations, and Modes can cross Spaces. Preserves the Move as primary, with no required `Active Space` / `Active Mode` headings or demand for Notion properties. Does not turn correction of labels into the main output.
