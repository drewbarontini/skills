# Learning Before the Next Attempt

Illustrative comparison for a team discussing its review rhythm. These are hypothetical mechanisms, not evidence that the source claim holds for every team.

## Source Idea

“Feedback only helps when it arrives before the next attempt.”

## Structural Core

An action produces information that can change a later action. When the information arrives after another similar action, that intervening action cannot incorporate the correction, although later actions still can.

## Candidate Analogies

### 1. Adjusting a Cake Batch

**Mapping**

- Work attempt → baking one batch of cakes.
- Review → tasting the baked result.
- Next attempt → mixing the next batch.
- Delayed feedback → mixing several batches before tasting the first.
- Repeated mistake → carrying the same ingredient imbalance into those batches.

**What it reveals**

Tasting the first batch cannot change batter already mixed, but it can still improve subsequent batches. The useful boundary is when the next action becomes difficult to revise.

**Where it breaks**

Work is often more reversible than mixed batter. Different tasks may also need different corrections rather than repeating one recipe.

### 2. Practicing a Musical Phrase

**Mapping**

- Work attempt → playing a phrase.
- Feedback → listening to a recording of that phrase.
- Next attempt → repeating it.
- Delayed review → many repetitions before listening.
- Correction → adjusting timing on a later repetition.

**What it reveals**

Repeated attempts create opportunities to apply feedback. Delayed review misses some of those opportunities without making future improvement impossible.

**Where it breaks**

A player may hear and correct errors while performing. Team feedback may come from someone else and concern a different kind of judgment.

### 3. Correcting a Walking Route

**Mapping**

- Work attempt → choosing a turn.
- Feedback → checking the route after that turn.
- Next attempt → choosing the next turn.
- Delayed feedback → walking through several junctions before checking.
- Rework → retracing steps to return to the route.

**What it reveals**

A later correction still helps, but more decisions may have accumulated around the earlier mistake. Delay can increase the cost of adjustment.

**Where it breaks**

The route assumes a known destination. Product work may use feedback to reconsider the destination itself.

## Strongest Analogy

**Adjusting a Cake Batch**

For a team discussing review timing, this makes the missed opportunity concrete: review one result before committing the same choice across several attempts. It also exposes the source claim's overly absolute “only.”

## Pattern Refinement

**Original formulation**

Feedback only helps when it arrives before the next attempt.

**What the analogies revealed**

Late feedback can still change future actions. What it loses is the chance to inform intervening actions; the cost also depends on whether those actions remain reversible.

**Refined formulation**

When feedback arrives after further similar actions, those actions miss an opportunity to incorporate it; later actions can still benefit, and the cost of delay depends on reversibility.

This is a clearer candidate claim, not an established Pattern. Real examples and counterexamples would be needed to assess it.

## Memorable Expression

Taste before repeating the batch.
