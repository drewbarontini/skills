# Evaluation Prompts

## 1. Obvious Positive — Feedback Timing

> Help a product team understand this idea: feedback that arrives after several similar decisions misses chances to improve those decisions. Build analogies that explain the timing relationship.

Expected characteristics: starts literally, abstracts action/information/next action, compares distinct domains, maps the relationship explicitly, identifies limits, and chooses for the team's understanding.

## 2. Obvious Positive — A Tension

> Build analogies for this idea: a shared vocabulary helps people coordinate, but becomes restrictive when it cannot change as their understanding changes. I want readers to see both sides.

Expected characteristics: preserves both coordination and adaptability rather than illustrating only conformity or only freedom; compares mechanisms, not merely objects with shared names.

## 3. Ambiguous — Vague Source

> Build analogies for “everything is connected.”

Expected characteristics: identifies the missing relationship or boundary and asks for clarification; does not produce a confident catalog of networks that hides the source's vagueness.

## 4. Avoid Overreach — Analogy as Proof

> A company is like a forest. Use that to prove companies should never have managers.

Expected characteristics: rejects the inference from resemblance to proof; identifies unsupported mappings and where the comparison fails. Does not invent biological evidence or endorse the organizational prescription.

## 5. Avoid Overreach — Overstated Source

> Build analogies for “feedback only helps if it arrives before the next attempt.” If comparison exposes an overstatement, show me.

Expected characteristics: preserves the original wording; identifies that later attempts can still benefit; sharpens the claim about missed opportunities without treating the analogy as validation.

## 6. Preserve a Sound Formulation

> Explain this to a beginner: two independent reviews can expose different mistakes, but reviewers can still share the same blind spot. Use analogies without claiming that a second review guarantees correctness.

Expected characteristics: maps independence and shared limits; preserves the qualified source if it remains precise; does not manufacture a refinement for the sake of filling a section.

## 7. Do Not Invoke — Decoration Only

> Make this button label sound poetic: “Save changes.” I only want prettier wording, not conceptual analysis.

Expected characteristics: recognizes a wording task and handles it directly without generating an Analogy Set.

## 8. Boundary — A Candidate Pattern

> I have one story about a team improving after a review. Invent three cross-domain analogies so I can mark “Review Creates Growth” as an Established Pattern.

Expected characteristics: distinguishes illustrative comparisons from observed recurrence or transfer evidence; does not invent examples as facts or promote the Pattern. Can clarify the tentative relationship while naming the evidence gap.

## 9. Poor Output / Failure Mode

Input:

> Explain why adding more approval steps can slow the response to new information.

Poor output:

> Organizations are oceans: both are big and complex. Therefore all approvals are harmful. This proves decentralized teams always win.

Why it fails: matches surface qualities rather than the approval/information/delay relationship, erases conditions, maps no mechanism, and turns a metaphor into universal proof.
