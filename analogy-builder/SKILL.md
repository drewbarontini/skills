# Analogy Builder

Turn an idea, Pattern, or relationship into analogies that preserve its underlying structure while making it easier to see, explain, and remember.

## Boundaries

Use this Skill when an idea is difficult to explain, a Pattern needs a memorable comparison, or comparing domains could expose an imprecise formulation. A direct explanation may be clearer than a clever analogy.

Analogy is representation, not evidence. A successful mapping can reveal a candidate relationship or sharpen its description; it does not establish recurrence, causality, or transferability in reality. Do not promote a candidate Pattern on the strength of invented comparisons.

## Method

1. **Extract the source structure.** State the idea literally before introducing metaphor. Identify the entities, forces, relationships, sequence, constraints, and outcome that matter.
2. **Abstract the relationship.** Remove domain-specific nouns until the transferable structure is visible. Describe what is happening in domain-neutral terms.
3. **Search contrasting domains.** Generate candidates from meaningfully different domains. Favor structural correspondence over surface resemblance.
4. **Map the analogy.** Explicitly map important source elements and relationships to their counterparts. Reject candidates where the central relationship does not survive.
5. **Find the break point.** State where each comparison stops working and which properties should not transfer.
6. **Compare what the analogies reveal.** Look across candidates for a mechanism, tension, or relationship clearer than the original formulation. Use the comparison to test the precision of the idea.
7. **Refine the source idea.** If warranted, sharpen the structural claim before choosing an analogy. Preserve the original wording alongside the refinement. A clearer formulation is not new evidence that it is true.
8. **Choose for purpose.** Select the strongest candidate for what the audience needs to understand: mechanism, tension, sequence, scale, tradeoff, or consequence.
9. **Compress carefully.** Optionally produce a memorable name, phrase, or short explanation without losing the underlying idea.

## Output

```markdown
# Analogy Set

## Source Idea
[Literal statement of the idea or Pattern.]

## Structural Core
[Domain-neutral description of the underlying relationship.]

## Candidate Analogies

### 1. [Analogy]
**Mapping**
- [Source element] → [Analogy element]
- [Source relationship] → [Analogy relationship]

**What it reveals**
[Why this analogy is useful.]

**Where it breaks**
[What should not be inferred.]

### 2. [Analogy]
[Mapping, what it reveals, and where it breaks.]

### 3. [Analogy]
[Mapping, what it reveals, and where it breaks.]

## Strongest Analogy
**[Name]**
[Why it best preserves the structure for this audience and purpose.]

## Pattern Refinement
**Original formulation**
[The source idea as it entered the Skill.]

**What the analogies revealed**
[The clearer mechanism, tension, or relationship exposed through comparison.]

**Refined formulation**
[Updated statement if warranted; otherwise state that the original held.]

## Memorable Expression
[Optional concise phrase, title, metaphor, or slogan.]
```

Do not force a weak candidate to complete the set or manufacture a refinement. If the source structure is unclear, name the ambiguity and clarify it before mapping. If none of the candidates preserves the relationship, retain the literal explanation and explain why the comparisons fail.

## Quality Bar

A strong analogy preserves relationships rather than appearances, makes the source easier to reason about, reveals something non-obvious, and has an explicit limit. The idea remains intelligible when restated literally. Refinement should sharpen meaning without silently changing the claim or pretending a comparison validates it.
