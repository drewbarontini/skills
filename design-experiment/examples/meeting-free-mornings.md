# Meeting-Free Mornings

## Inquiry

Does protecting two mornings each week improve sustained attention on difficult creative work for this team without delaying necessary coordination?

## Assertion

Two meeting-free mornings will increase uninterrupted creative work, but may shift coordination costs into the afternoon.

## Test

For three weeks, protect Tuesday and Thursday mornings from internal meetings for one product team. Keep urgent incident response available and do not change the rest of the meeting schedule.

## Observable Signals

- Team members' brief weekly account of focus quality and coordination friction.
- Completed blocks of planned creative work, interpreted alongside scope and difficulty.
- Meetings displaced, delayed decisions, and urgent exceptions.

## Boundaries

- Duration: three weeks.
- Scope: one product team and internal meetings only.
- Stop condition: end early if customer commitments or incident response are materially harmed.

## Learning Map

- If focus improves without meaningful coordination delay, the team has evidence to test a sustainable Rhythm.
- If focus improves but delays rise, adjust the protected window or coordination mechanism.
- If neither focus nor coordination changes, revisit whether meetings were the relevant constraint.
- If evidence is mixed, run a narrower follow-up rather than declaring success or failure.

## Next

Review the Assertion after three weeks, then end the Experiment, revise it, or consider turning the supported conditions into a Practice and Rhythm.
