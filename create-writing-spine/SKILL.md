# Create Writing Spine

Interview an author to make the central argument and narrative progression of a Work visible **before drafting**. The result is a small Writing Spine, not an outline or an article. Preserve the author's thinking, phrasing, uncertainty, and judgment.

## Inputs and Boundaries

Start with the author's observations, experiences, prior conversations, rough notes, or an existing Work. Read a specifically supplied, accessible Work or Inquiry before interviewing. Never invent the author's experiences, evidence, or convictions.

The skill develops an argument. Use Frame Inquiry when the author instead needs to investigate what is true. Analogy Builder can optionally test or illuminate a relationship but must not be a required step or output section.

## Method

1. **Surface the idea.** Ask about a concrete observation, surprising experience, unresolved tension, or emerging connection. Find out what the author noticed and why it matters without immediately imposing a thesis.
2. **Connect the claims.** Reflect provisional claims in the author's language. Work toward three declarative claims that advance one argument. Test *why* each follows from the previous one. Surface unresolved assumptions or missing links instead of inventing continuity.
3. **Compress the spine.** Once the author has a defensible throughline and is ready, keep a working name, a two- or three-sentence Core Idea, and three declarative claims. Give each claim exactly three **unlabeled** short bullets in this order: supporting idea, reader takeaway, and arrow-marked transition. The last transition points toward the closing thought, not a fourth claim.

## Interview Behavior

- Ask **one meaningful question at a time** and adapt to the answer, not a fixed questionnaire.
- Distinguish the author's confirmed position from your own tentative interpretation.
- Make implicit transitions explicit and challenge gaps respectfully.
- Use stories, analogies, or examples to explore the thought if offered, but do not mandate or append them.
- Three claims are a useful constraint, not permission to fabricate a weak third claim. Keep an open question unresolved when necessary.
- Stop asking once the argument is ready; the interview may be rich, but its output must stay brief.

## Output

Use the Writing Spine structure below, keeping **Core Idea** and **Three Claims** as Title Case headings. The claims are complete declarative sentences. The three bullets are always in the same order but have **no displayed labels**; use an arrow on the third bullet for clarity.

```markdown
# [Working Name]

## Core Idea
[Two or three sentences expressing the central argument and why it matters.]

## Three Claims

### 1. [First declarative claim.]
- [What this claim establishes.]
- [What the reader should take away.]
- → [Why the second claim follows.]

### 2. [Second declarative claim.]
- [What this claim develops or reveals.]
- [What the reader should take away.]
- → [What leads to the third claim.]

### 3. [Third declarative claim.]
- [What this claim resolves or establishes.]
- [The lasting insight or implication.]
- → [How this leads into the close.]
```

Keep each bullet to one short sentence or phrase, not an explanatory paragraph. The third bullet under the final claim provides a bridge to the conclusion instead of a fourth claim.

When working in Notion, use the existing Work's **Name** property for its title; omit the duplicate title heading from the page body. Preserve unrelated properties, relations, and prose. Save only when requested, and never replace published prose with a working scaffold.

## Quality Bar

A strong Writing Spine is faithful to the author's reasoning, specific enough to guide writing, and readable as a coherent progression without the article. The Core Idea is two or three sentences, and each of three declarative claims has three concise unlabeled bullets: idea, takeaway, and transition. No separate paragraphs, examples, analogy sections, or drafting plan are required. Do not smooth over uncertainty to make the structure appear complete.
