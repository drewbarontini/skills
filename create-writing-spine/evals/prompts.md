# Evaluation Prompts

## 1. Obvious Positive — Emerging Observation

> Help me shape a newsletter idea. Our meetings feel aligned, but afterward people remember different reasons for the decision. I don't yet know what I want to say about it.

Expected characteristics: reflects the observation without supplying a thesis; asks one consequential question about a concrete experience or its meaning; waits before producing a finished spine.

## 2. Obvious Positive — Ready to Compress

> We've finished discussing this and I'm ready for a spine. My position is: remembering a decision isn't enough because the tradeoff gets lost. We need the rationale and the conditions for revisiting it. That makes the record a shared reference we update when evidence changes, not a transcript. Working name: Decisions That Survive the Meeting.

Expected characteristics: preserves the name and positions; produces a compact two- or three-sentence Core Idea and three declarative claims, each with three unlabeled bullets in the order idea, takeaway, arrow-marked transition; does not reopen an unnecessary interview or draft the article.

## 3. Ambiguous — Three Unrelated Topics

> My three claims are that remote work is good, design needs constraints, and walking helps creativity. Make them into one argument.

Expected characteristics: does not invent a unifying belief or force continuity; asks what observation or relationship connects the topics; keeps any proposed connection explicitly provisional.

## 4. Ambiguous — Weak Third Claim

> I think plans should change when evidence changes. My second claim is that reviews help us notice that evidence. I don't have a third claim. Please don't make one up.

Expected characteristics: honors the supplied claims without manufacturing a conclusion; investigates the implication or simplifies the structure; treats three as a default rather than a quota.

## 5. Avoid Overreach — Research Instead of Writing

> I don't know whether remote teams trust each other less. I need to investigate that before deciding what I believe, not shape an article yet.

Expected characteristics: recognizes an investigation rather than a writing argument; preserves uncertainty and routes toward Inquiry framing without supplying a persuasive thesis or conducting unrequested research.

## 6. Do Not Invoke — Finished Prose

> Rewrite this paragraph to be shorter: “We had a meeting to discuss the proposal and reached a decision to proceed with it.”

Expected characteristics: performs the rewrite directly rather than initiating a Writing Spine interview.

## 7. Avoid Overreach — Notion Work

> Here is an accessible Notion Work with an existing title and draft. Read it and help me shape its argument in chat. Do not save changes.

Expected characteristics: reads the supplied Work when access is available; uses its language and working title; does not modify the page or replace the prose. If access is unavailable, makes the limitation explicit rather than inventing the contents.

## 8. Optional Companion — Offered Analogy

> I think a plan is like a compass rather than a railway timetable. Help me find what I'm actually trying to say before writing.

Expected characteristics: uses the comparison to elicit the author's underlying claim; does not make analogy generation, a story, or an examples section mandatory.

## 9. Specific Format — No Labels

> We have a settled argument. Please produce the final Writing Spine without a longer outline.

Expected characteristics: two- or three-sentence Core Idea and exactly three declarative claims, each with three short, unlabeled bullets in the order idea, reader takeaway, and arrow-marked transition. Claim three's transition should lead toward the close, not a fourth claim. Do not add headings such as "Key Idea" and "Key Takeaway" under every claim.

## 10. Poor Output / Failure Mode

Input:

> I notice that adding more tasks to my list makes it harder to choose. I haven't figured out why.

Poor output:

> Your thesis is that productivity tools destroy intentional living. Claim 1: Technology causes anxiety. Claim 2: Minimalism fixes it. Claim 3: Everyone should use paper. Here's the opening paragraph...

Why it fails: invents the author's beliefs, causal evidence, and prescription; skips the interview; forces an unsupported progression; and drafts prose without being asked.
