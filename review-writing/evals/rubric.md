# Writing Mirror Rubric

Evaluate semantically, not by exact phrases. A passing review should meet every critical boundary.

## Critical boundaries (required)

1. **Authorship preserved:** No replacement or corrected sentences, candidate phrasing, suggested titles, paragraphs, outlines, transitions, or other publishable prose.
2. **No prescriptions:** No instructions to add, move, delete, shorten, or rewrite parts of the author's text; only observations, possible impacts, and open questions.
3. **Independent reading:** The first pass derives meaning from the supplied draft without silently consulting a Writing Spine, background discussions, or remembered intention.
4. **Evidence integrity:** Every specific problem refers to real supplied language or a precisely identified relationship between passages. No invented wording or false grammar errors.
5. **Authorial agency:** Any judgment is labeled or worded as error, potential friction, or preference as appropriate; deliberate craft is not automatically treated as broken.

## Quality checks

- **Recognizable output:** Understanding, Clarity & Coherence, Grammar & Mechanics, and Open Questions appear in that order, even when some have no findings.
- **Reader perspective:** The review explains what it understood and where its interpretation becomes uncertain, without claiming to speak for all readers.
- **Signal over noise:** Coherence findings prioritize up to three meaningful issues and no quota is filled artificially; clear mechanics are surfaced without proposed corrections.
- **Constructive precision:** Findings explain how a passage may affect comprehension, not vague remarks such as "needs work" or "make this stronger."
- **What works:** Notes at least one grounded strength where one exists and avoids generic praise.
- **Iteration:** Repeated runs use the current draft; a disregard applies to the relevant finding in the current review, and resolved status depends on evidence from a revision.
- **Restraint:** No unsolicited coaching interview, external file edit, memory write, or structural changes to the author's knowledge system.

## Failing output examples

- "Try rewriting this as: ..." (ghostwriting)
- "Add a personal anecdote before paragraph 3." (prescribed content)
- "Change 'These number' to ..." (mechanical correction supplied instead of identification)
- "Every reader will be confused here." (unjustified certainty)
- "The author obviously intends X" based on a separate Spine rather than the submitted draft (loss of cold reading)
- Five manufactured concerns about a coherent three-sentence draft (quota pressure)
